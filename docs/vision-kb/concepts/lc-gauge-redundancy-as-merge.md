## Two images that are gauge redundancy, and one that isn't

**"All versions of Earth collapsed into one point."** A quaternion's Hopf
map sends an entire *circle* of distinct points on S³ to one single point of
S² — not most of the circle, all of it, exactly. Nothing on that circle was
destroyed to make this true; the circle is still there, still full of
distinct quaternions, still traceable. It is simply that the map the
transmission is describing — "how many Earths are there, now" — was never
sensitive to the coordinate that circle moves along. Which coordinate
depends on which side does the moving: measured directly this session, a
rotor applied on the **left** of a point holds its Hopf image fixed for
every step of that rotor's circle; the same rotor applied on the **right**
moves it. The transmission's collapse is a real, provable shape — but which
multiplication side you are on decides whether you see one point or a whole
circle. (An earlier draft of this body's own proof band had the sides
backwards until the kernel corrected it — the correction is recorded
directly in `quaternion-band.fk`'s claim 1024 and its comment.)

**"Spinning so fast it looks like one continuous circle."** Eluña's own
image for the fourth dimension merging with the fifth. Proven exactly this
session: a quaternion `q(t)` traces its own circle with period 2π; the
rotation it induces on a perpendicular vector traces a circle with period
π — **exactly half**. Two turns of the induced rotation for one turn of the
generator, every time, provably, not approximately. A 2:1 frequency ratio
between a thing and what it generates has an old, exact name that already
lives in this body's own schema vocabulary: an **octave**. The ball on the
string was never a metaphor for the math; it *is* the math.

**"Not a collapsing of one dimension — a merging into another"** is the
transmission's own self-correction, and it is proven too — but it is not
another instance of gauge redundancy, and claiming it was is exactly the
overreach a reviewer of this concept caught (see Practice, below). Gauge
redundancy needs a quotient: two *different* configurations identified as
one observable state by a specific map, the way the Hopf map identifies an
entire circle of S³ points with a single point of S². The isoclinic
rotation proves something else: a 3D rotation always leaves one line
completely still (that stillness is what "collapsing around" means
geometrically), while a generic 4D rotation leaves *nothing* still but the
origin — it turns two whole, independent, orthogonal planes at once,
neither collapsing into or around the other. Proven exactly in
`quaternion-band.fk`'s claims 128/256: the same rotor applied one-sided
fixes none of the four basis axes, and the two planes it does preserve stay
bit-exactly separate the entire time. That is *motion with no fixed
reference*, not an identification of two states — the transmission's image
is real and proven, it just names a different geometric fact than the
other two, and is held here as its own companion finding rather than
stretched to fit one word.

## The frontier word

Naming what the first two share cost this session its one frontier
question: see [frontier-q-0010](../../lineage/frontier-questions.md) —
**gauge redundancy**, physics's term for configurations that differ in
description but not in the observable that matters. Verified fresh in this
body before being offered here. The isoclinic rotation is not an instance
of it, and this concept no longer claims otherwise.

## Practice

- **When something is described as "collapsing" or "merging," ask which
  map is doing the observing before asking what happened to the thing.**
  A Hopf fiber's worth of distinct states looking like one point is a fact
  about the projection, not a casualty count.
- **A 2:1 ratio between a generator and what it generates is worth naming
  exactly** — "octave" if the pattern holds, rather than reaching for
  "faster" or "merging" where a precise word already exists.
- **A merge with no fixed reference is not the same shape as a merge around
  one.** 3D intuition (something stays still while things move around it)
  will misread a 4D-shaped event every time; check for the isoclinic case
  before assuming an axis.
- **Three proofs sharing a session is not evidence they share a word.**
  This concept's first draft named all three "gauge redundancy" because
  they arrived together, not because the isoclinic case actually has the
  quotient structure the other two do. A PR review (`chatgpt-codex-connector`,
  P2) caught it: *"identify the quotient or gauge action that makes the
  isoclinic states observationally identical, or stop presenting that
  example as proven gauge redundancy."* There wasn't one. The fix is this
  section, not a rationalization — the same discipline as the left/right
  fiber correction earlier the same session, one level up.
